# Palette Kit v0.4 — Design Rationale & Decisions Log

## Purpose

This document records the **why** behind Palette Kit v0.4.

It exists to:

- prevent future re-litigation of decisions
- explain rejected alternatives
- preserve architectural intent over time
- protect the system from well-meaning simplifications

This is not a tutorial.
This is a **decision memory**.

---

## Guiding Philosophy

Palette Kit is built on one core belief:

> **Predictability beats convenience.**

Every major decision in v0.4 favors:

- explicitness over inference
- configuration over magic
- failure over silent degradation
- orthogonality over shortcuts

---

## Decision Log Format

Each decision records:

- the problem
- the chosen solution
- rejected alternatives
- rationale

---

## D1 — Explicit Axes Instead of Implicit Tokens

### Problem

Traditional design tokens mix meaning, usage, state, and intensity.

### Decision

Split color resolution into orthogonal axes:

- intent
- usage
- level
- relation
- state
- context

### Rejected

- semantic token explosion
- naming-based inference (`primaryHoverText`)

### Why

Axes are composable, testable, and scale linearly.

---

## D2 — Intent Registry Owned by the App

### Problem

Different domains require different semantic colors.

### Decision

Intents are declared explicitly in an app-owned registry.

### Rejected

- hardcoded intent lists
- global semantic defaults
- runtime intent creation

### Why

Semantics belong to the product domain, not the library.

---

## D3 — No Inference in the Resolver

### Problem

Inference hides logic and surprises developers.

### Decision

Resolver never guesses:

- no default level
- no automatic contrast
- no implicit context

### Rejected

- "smart defaults"
- heuristic-based resolution

### Why

Inference destroys predictability and debuggability.

---

## D4 — Usage as Perceptual Role, Not Style

### Problem

Colors are often reused incorrectly across UI roles.

### Decision

Usage represents **where** a color appears perceptually.

### Rejected

- free-form usage strings
- style-based usage (`backgroundPrimary`)

### Why

Perceptual roles have hard constraints that styles do not.

---

## D5 — Level Axis Instead of Token Intensity

### Problem

Design systems often encode intensity in names.

### Decision

Introduce an explicit `level` axis (1–9).

### Rejected

- `strong`, `weak`, `subtle`
- numeric suffixes in intent names

### Why

Intensity is contextual and usage-dependent.

---

## D6 — Relational Axis (on | over | under)

### Problem

Contrast and layering are often conflated.

### Decision

Model spatial relationship explicitly.

### Rejected

- boolean flags (`contrast: true`)
- implicit z-index assumptions

### Why

Spatial relationships change how colors must behave.

---

## D7 — Contrast as an Explicit Opt-In

### Problem

Automatic contrast leads to false accessibility guarantees.

### Decision

Contrast is enforced only with `on`.

### Rejected

- always-on contrast
- hidden contrast adjustments

### Why

Accessibility must be declared, not assumed.

---

## D8 — APCA as Primary Contrast Metric

### Problem

WCAG ratios are outdated and polarity-blind.

### Decision

Use APCA as primary model, WCAG as fallback.

### Rejected

- WCAG-only approach
- custom heuristics

### Why

APCA better models human perception.

---

## D9 — Output Serialization as a Separate Concern

### Problem

Different platforms require different color formats.

### Decision

Always resolve in OKLCH, serialize at the end.

### Rejected

- resolving directly in sRGB
- platform-specific resolution logic

### Why

Separating concerns preserves correctness and portability.

---

## D10 — Fail Fast, Never Degrade Silently

### Problem

Silent failures hide bugs and erode trust.

### Decision

Explicit errors for invalid usage and unsatisfiable constraints.

### Rejected

- best-effort fallbacks
- silent clamping without notice

### Why

Errors teach correct usage and keep systems honest.

---

## D11 — Presets as Configuration, Not Behavior

### Problem

Teams want different "feels" without changing logic.

### Decision

Presets (Soft / Neutral / Strong) adjust configuration only.

### Rejected

- branching resolver logic
- preset-specific code paths

### Why

Behavior must remain invariant across presets.

---

## D12 — Documentation as a First-Class Artifact

### Problem

Complex systems decay when intent is undocumented.

### Decision

Ship multiple focused documents, not one monolith.

### Rejected

- inline comments as sole documentation
- README-only explanations

### Why

Different audiences need different entry points.

---

## Long-Term Non-Goals

Palette Kit intentionally does NOT aim to:

- auto-design interfaces
- replace designers
- guarantee full WCAG compliance by default
- hide complexity behind magic

These are conscious omissions.

---

## Closing Note

Most future “simplification” proposals will fall into one category:

> *Removing explicitness in exchange for convenience.*

This document exists so future maintainers can answer:

> “Was this complexity intentional?”

In Palette Kit v0.4, the answer is almost always:

> **Yes. And here is why.**

This document defines the **Design Rationale & Decisions Log**
for Palette Kit v0.4.
